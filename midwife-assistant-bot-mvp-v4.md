# Midwife Assistant — MVP Plan v4 (Co-pilot Model)

## What Changed From v3

v3 described an **autonomous bot** that auto-replied to clients under a confidence threshold and escalated edge cases to a human. v4 inverts this: the AI is a **co-pilot**, not an autonomous agent. Every outbound message — without exception — passes through the midwife's hands before reaching the client. The Vue admin, previously a fallback surface for escalations, becomes the **primary daily-use surface** for the midwife.

This single change cascades through the entire architecture: simpler safety story, lower regulatory exposure, easier liability posture, simpler state machine, and a clearer value proposition for both sides of the marketplace. We trade the "magic" of instant auto-replies for trust, defensibility, and a viable path to multi-midwife scale.

Client onboarding moves to **Pattern 1 (Click-to-Chat)**: midwife shares a tokenized `wa.me` link with each new client. Client taps, sends the first message, and the WhatsApp 24-hour service window opens naturally — sidestepping Meta template approval friction for first contact.

---

## Product Vision

**The two-sided problem.** Pregnant, birthing, and postpartum women have nowhere safe to ask the dozens of small but high-anxiety questions that arise daily ("Is this normal?"). The default answer — Google — is uncertain, ungrounded, and often misleading at the worst possible moment of a person's life. Midwives, on the other hand, hold the knowledge to answer 99% of these questions confidently, but are flooded with the same questions across many clients and have no efficient way to share that knowledge at scale.

**The co-pilot premise.** Give each client a 24/7 WhatsApp channel to her midwife. Behind that channel, an AI assistant drafts a grounded reply for every incoming message — citing the midwife's own past knowledge — and presents it to the midwife in a PWA inbox. The midwife taps Approve, Edit, or Write Own. The client experiences a single, fast, trusted channel with her midwife. The midwife experiences a knowledge-amplified inbox where 80% of replies are one tap.

**The economics.** Client pays a monthly fee for 24h access. Midwife earns a share once volume justifies onboarding her. The AI is the leverage that makes the unit economics work.

## Architectural Principles & Design Patterns

The architecture is shaped by nine deliberate patterns. Each exists to address a specific risk or requirement of operating an AI-assisted system in a healthcare-adjacent, regulated, two-sided market.

### 1. Human-in-the-Loop (Co-pilot Pattern) — *defining pattern*

Every outbound message to a client is gated by midwife approval. The AI never has the capability to autonomously send. This is enforced at the architecture level: the WhatsApp adapter's `send_message()` is only callable from the `admin.reply` route, never from the inbound message handler.

**Why:** Medical-adjacent content cannot tolerate even rare hallucinations or context errors. The legal posture of "AI sent it autonomously with 0.7 confidence" is indefensible; "AI drafted, midwife reviewed and sent" is professionally normal. Liability stays with the licensed practitioner where it belongs.

**Trade-off:** Reply latency depends on midwife responsiveness, not system speed. We accept this and design the midwife's experience to make approval as fast as a WhatsApp tap.

### 2. Ports & Adapters (Hexagonal Architecture)

The core business logic — retrieval, generation, conversation state, knowledge management — is isolated from the messaging channel. WhatsApp lives behind a `MessageChannel` interface in `adapters/`. The core logic doesn't know what WhatsApp is.

**Why:** The messaging surface will evolve. Messenger, Telegram, in-app chat, possibly Signal. Each is a new adapter, not a rewrite. This also enables headless testing of core logic without WhatsApp involvement.

### 3. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Drafts are generated by Gemini grounded in retrieved snippets from Vertex AI Search. The system prompt requires the model to answer *only* from provided reference material and to cite sources. The midwife sees the cited snippets alongside the draft and can verify provenance before approving.

**Why:** Ungrounded LLM generation hallucinates. In medical content, hallucination is unacceptable. RAG transforms the LLM from "knowledge source" (unsafe) to "natural-language renderer of known content" (safer).

### 4. Fail-Closed Safety

Any ambiguity — low retrieval confidence, emergency triage signal, downstream API failure, missing context — surfaces the message to the midwife with no draft, flagged as urgent. There is no "best-effort auto-reply" path. The system's default behavior under uncertainty is to escalate.

A first-pass safety classifier (LLM-based, not keyword-based — keywords miss too much in pregnancy/birth contexts where descriptions are oblique) tags inbound messages on a 4-level urgency scale. High-urgency messages bypass the drafting step entirely and push a priority notification to the midwife.

**Why:** Default-deny is the only acceptable posture when the failure mode is delayed response to a real emergency.

### 5. Event Sourcing for Audit

Firestore holds current conversation state (mutable, queryable). Every message in and out is *additionally* written to an append-only audit log (Cloud Logging → write-once GCS bucket with object retention lock). Reconstructing any conversation at any historical moment is possible from the audit log alone.

**Why:** Healthcare-adjacent operations face regulatory scrutiny and dispute resolution. Mutable state is insufficient evidence. We need an immutable record of *what the AI drafted, what the midwife approved, and what the client received,* timestamped and tamper-evident.

### 6. PWA-First Midwife Surface (Single-Artifact Deployment)

The midwife's primary tool is a Vue 3 PWA — installable to the home screen, push-notification enabled, indistinguishable from a native app in daily use. The PWA bundle is built into the same Docker image as the FastAPI backend and served as static files from a single Cloud Run service.

**Why PWA over native:** Single codebase, no app store review latency, push notifications via Web Push API (iOS 16.4+ supports them when installed to home screen), instant deployment of UI changes. For a pilot with one midwife, the constraints of PWA push on iOS are acceptable.

**Why single artifact:** Atomic deploys, one URL, one set of secrets, one Cloud Run service to monitor. Cheap to split into separate services later if scale demands.

### 7. Token-Based Onboarding (Pattern 1)

The midwife adds a new client in the PWA. The system generates a unique tokenized `wa.me` link (e.g., `wa.me/{biz_number}?text=Hi+I'm+inv_a8f3...`). The midwife shares the link with the client through her preferred existing channel. Client taps, sends the prefilled hello, bot matches the token to the pending invite record and attaches the client profile.

**Why:** Bypasses Meta's template-approval requirement for first contact (templates needed only for re-engagement after the 24h window closes, in Pattern 2 territory). Provides verifiable opt-in evidence — the client physically initiated the conversation. Cheaper (no outbound template billing for onboarding). Matches how midwives already meet clients (offline first, digital handoff second).

### 8. Two-Tier Knowledge Base

When the midwife approves and sends a reply, she can optionally tag it as "valuable for the knowledge base." Tagged Q&A pairs go to a **staging index**, not directly to production. A weekly review queue surfaces staged chunks for the midwife (or a designated reviewer) to promote, edit, or discard before they enter the production retrieval index.

**Why:** Instant ingestion creates two failure modes: (1) the midwife approves a context-specific reply that shouldn't generalize ("yes that's fine *for you* given your history"), and (2) the same Q&A gets re-ingested dozens of times with minor variations, polluting retrieval ranking. The weekly gate adds latency but prevents silent KB drift in medical content.

### 9. Compliance-by-Design

Three primitives are first-class in the architecture, not afterthoughts:

- **AI disclosure.** The first bot message to every new client is the consent + disclosure prompt. The midwife's PWA shows an "AI-drafted" badge on every draft, so she's always aware. Optionally, outbound replies are marked at the message level as "AI-assisted" in metadata (visible in audit log even if not in the client chat).
- **Consent capture.** Stored per client as message ID + timestamp + verbatim consent reply ("YES"). Retrievable in audit log.
- **Data minimization & retention.** No clinical history stored beyond conversation messages and a minimal client profile (name, due date, midwife reference). Right-to-be-forgotten flow wipes Firestore + KB references but retains audit log under GDPR Art. 17(3)(e) (legal claims).

## Conversation Flows

### Flow 1: Client Onboarding (Pattern 1)

```
Midwife in PWA → [+ Add Client]
  → enters: name, phone, due date
  → system creates ClientInvite record (token, status=pending)
  → PWA displays unique wa.me link + QR code
  → midwife shares link via her existing channel (her personal WhatsApp,
    email, in-person QR scan)

Client taps link
  → WhatsApp opens with prefilled "Hi I'm <name>, invite <token>"
  → client sends
  → webhook receives, matches token, links phone to client record
  → bot sends consent message:
      "Hi <name>! This is <Midwife>'s channel. An AI assistant
       drafts replies, which <Midwife> personally reviews before
       sending. Reply YES to confirm you understand and consent.
       (You can stop or request human-only replies anytime by
       writing STOP or HUMAN.)"

Client replies YES
  → consent record stored (message id + timestamp + verbatim)
  → state transitions to active
  → midwife sees in PWA: "<name> is now onboarded"
```

### Flow 2: Inbound Message (Active Client)

```
Client sends message
  → webhook receives
  → audit log: inbound recorded
  → safety.py classifies urgency (LLM, 4 levels)
  
  IF urgency == HIGH:
    → no draft generated
    → push notification to midwife (priority)
    → PWA inbox highlights item in red, no draft suggested

  ELSE:
    → retriever.py queries production Vertex AI Search index
    → generator.py: Gemini grounded draft with citations
    → audit log: draft recorded (NOT sent)
    → push notification to midwife (normal priority)
    → PWA inbox shows draft + cited KB snippets
```

### Flow 3: Midwife Review & Send

```
Midwife opens PWA (or taps push notification)
  → sees draft card with:
      - client message
      - urgency badge
      - AI-generated draft
      - cited KB snippets (collapsible)
      - [Approve & Send] [Edit] [Write Own] [Tag for KB after sending]

  Approve & Send:
    → POST /api/admin/conversations/{id}/reply with draft body
    → adapters/whatsapp.py sends via WhatsApp API
    → audit log: outbound recorded (with "AI-drafted, midwife-approved" tag)
    → state: awaiting_client

  Edit:
    → textarea pre-filled with draft; midwife modifies; sends
    → audit log: "AI-drafted, midwife-edited"

  Write Own:
    → blank textarea; midwife writes from scratch; sends
    → audit log: "midwife-original"

  Tag for KB:
    → Q&A pair (client question + final approved reply) queued to staging index
    → appears in next week's KB review batch
```

### Flow 4: 24-Hour Window Handling

```
On every outbound, store last_client_inbound_at on the client record.

On midwife reply attempt:
  IF (now - last_client_inbound_at) > 24h:
    → freeform send blocked
    → PWA shows: "24h window closed. Send a check-in template
       to reopen the conversation."
    → midwife picks from approved template library (post-MVP for true
       initiation; for MVP, freeform restricted to active window only)
```

## Admin API (Pure JSON, Bearer-token protected)

```
# Clients & onboarding
POST   /api/admin/clients                    → create client + generate invite link
GET    /api/admin/clients                    → list clients
GET    /api/admin/clients/{id}               → client profile + state
POST   /api/admin/clients/{id}/archive       → archive client

# Conversations
GET    /api/admin/conversations              → inbox: pending drafts + urgent
GET    /api/admin/conversations/{id}         → full thread + current draft
POST   /api/admin/conversations/{id}/reply   → send reply via WhatsApp adapter
                                                body: { text, source: "approved"|"edited"|"original" }
POST   /api/admin/conversations/{id}/tag_kb  → queue last Q&A to staging index

# KB review
GET    /api/admin/kb/staging                 → weekly review queue
POST   /api/admin/kb/staging/{id}/promote    → move to production index
POST   /api/admin/kb/staging/{id}/edit       → edit then promote
POST   /api/admin/kb/staging/{id}/discard

# Push subscriptions
POST   /api/admin/push/subscribe             → register Web Push subscription
DELETE /api/admin/push/subscribe             → unregister
```

## Open Risks (Track, Don't Hide)

1. **Regulatory classification.** Pre-launch lawyer review needed on: MDR Class I qualification under EU Regulation 2017/745; GDPR Art. 9 DPIA; EU AI Act Art. 50 transparency obligations. Treat as a hard gate before public pilot.

2. **Meta WhatsApp policy.** Healthcare-adjacent use cases face additional Meta review. Business verification + use-case approval can be slow or denied. Have a fallback plan (Telegram, dedicated web chat).

3. **iOS PWA push reliability.** iOS 16.4+ supports Web Push only when PWA is installed to home screen. If the pilot midwife uses Android, low risk; if iOS, validate push delivery early in week 3.

4. **Cold-start drops on webhook.** WhatsApp retries with backoff, but slow responses degrade UX. min-instances=1 mitigates; monitor p99 webhook latency from day one.

5. **KB quality from a single midwife's history.** Past chat exports may be inconsistent, context-stripped, or outdated. Manual review of the first 20 chunks (week 1) is non-negotiable. Plan to discard 30–50% of auto-extracted chunks.

6. **Bus factor.** One pilot midwife = no coverage. Define explicit escalation behavior when she's offline >2h on an active urgent item. Even MVP needs *something* — even if it's a backup phone number in the bot's "I'll get back to you" auto-acknowledgment.

7. **Pricing validation.** No paid customer has confirmed willingness to pay. Even with strong qualitative signal ("women will pay anything for peace of mind"), the MVP must include a payment mechanism — even a placeholder Stripe checkout — to convert anecdote into evidence.
